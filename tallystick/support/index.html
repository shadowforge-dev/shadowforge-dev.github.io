Support

Get in touch

Tallystick is made by one person. Email hello@shadowforge.dev and you will get a reply from him rather than from a queue.

Useful things to include: what game you were playing, what you expected to happen, and what happened instead. If a score came out wrong, the round it happened on helps a great deal.

My game isn't in the list

Search first — several games ship under a neutral name and answer to the one on the box. Typing "phase 10", "farkle" or "skyjo" will find them.

If it genuinely is not there, you can build it yourself: search for its name, and the empty result offers to make a game called that. Set how long it runs, whether high or low wins, and whether scores can go below zero. Or email and ask.

We play it differently

Most games ship with variants — Cribbage to 61, Golf over eighteen, Rummy to 300. Open a game's ⓘ and look under versions. You can also change tonight's target on the setup sheet without saving anything, or write down your house rules as a version of your own.

Somebody's score is wrong

Tap the number on the board and type the right one. Every total re-adds itself, including who is winning, however many rounds ago the mistake was. Finishing a game is not a one-way door either: correct a score after the end and the game carries on.

I bought Pro and it isn't showing

Open Settings and tap Tallystick Pro, then "Restore a previous purchase". Purchases are tied to the Apple Account that made them, so check you are signed in with the same one. Pro is a one-time unlock — it does not expire and it is not a subscription.

Moving to a new phone

An ordinary encrypted device backup carries your record across. If you are setting the new phone up fresh instead, open Settings → Your record → Save a copy on the old phone, send the file to the new one, and restore it there. Games still in progress are deliberately left out of that file — a half-played game restored a week later is worse than no game.

Nothing is sent anywhere

Tallystick has no account and makes no network requests. If you are wondering what it stores and where, the privacy policy says so plainly.
